A secret code for numbers

Meet Nyx. A quick story, then a couple of checks — before you touch a single button.

Shabnam is always 3 years older than Aftab. When Aftab was 10, Shabnam was 13. When Aftab is 23...

Aftab = 23
+3 →
Shabnam = 26

Instead of writing "Aftab's age" every single time, mathematicians use a letter — say a — to stand in for it. That's a letter-number.

Parthiv lines up matchstick L shapes. Every single L costs exactly 2 matchsticks — no more, no less.

However many Ls he builds — call that n — the sticks needed are always 2 × n. One tiny expression covers every possible n.

Writing × between a number and a letter gets old fast. So mathematicians quietly drop it.

4 × n → 4n

4 × n and 4n mean the exact same thing. Number always comes first, letter right after — no gap, no dot, no fuss.

That's the whole trick: swap a changing quantity for a letter, write the rule once, and it works for every value — 5, 50, or 5 million.
