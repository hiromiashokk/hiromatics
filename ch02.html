An expression is a little maths sentence made of numbers and operations. Let's learn to read them in the right order.

Let's spark some expressions to life and crack the order-of-operations code.

An expression mixes numbers with +, −, ×, ÷. For 30 marbles plus 5 bags of 4, you write 30 + 5 × 4. Each piece joined by + or − is called a term.

It's just like a shopping bill: ₹50 for a book plus 3 pens at ₹10 each → 50 + 3 × 10.

Joke: Why did the brackets always go first at the party? Because in maths, brackets ALWAYS go first! 🎉

Order matters! Do × and ÷ before + and −, and anything in brackets first.

So 30 + 5 × 4 = 30 + 20 = 50 — not 35 × 4. Multiply the 5 bags first, then add the loose marbles.

Get the order wrong and the answer changes completely, like adding sauce before you cook the pasta!

The distributive property says a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c. Picture a rectangle split into two blocks — the areas add up to the whole.

6 × 21 feels hard, but 6 × (20 + 1) = 120 + 6 = 126. Think of 6 rows of 21 chairs split into 20 + 1.

This split is the secret behind mental-math tricks: break a scary number into friendly pieces, multiply each, then add.

Fun fact: Shopkeepers do this in their heads all the time — 7 × 99 = 7 × 100 − 7 = 693, faster than a calculator! ⚡
Joke: Why was the equal sign so humble? It knew it wasn't greater OR less than anyone. 😌
