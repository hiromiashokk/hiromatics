There's a mystery number hiding in every equation. Let's learn to keep the scale balanced and reveal the unknown.

In the shadows — I'm Nyx.

Let's shine a light on the hidden number.

An equation says two sides are equal — just like a balanced weighing scale. The unknown (a letter such as x) is the mystery weight we want to find.

x + 3 = 7 means “some number plus 3 balances 7.”

Joke: Why did x go to the party alone? It was still an unknown! 😄

The golden rule: whatever you do to one side, do the same to the other — that keeps the scale level.

Take away 3 from both pans of x + 3 = 7 and you're left with x = 4.

To free the unknown, use inverse operations: undo + with −, and × with ÷.

So 2x = 10 becomes x = 10 ÷ 2 = 5. You divided both sides by 2.

Always check: put your answer back in. For x = 4, does 4 + 3 = 7? Yes — balanced!

Fun fact: The word “algebra” comes from an Arabic book title over 1,000 years old — people have been finding the unknown for a very long time! 🔮
Joke: Why was the equation so calm? It always did the same to both sides. ⚖️

Weigh the unknown

The chapter opens with a weighing scale: two pans, some numbers, one rule — if the scale is level, both sides weigh the same. Tap a known weight to lift it off both pans at once. The scale stays level the whole time — that is the entire trick to finding x.

x + 3 = 7

Solve It

Now do it without the pans — the same idea works on paper. Whatever you do to one side of an equation, do to the other, until only the letter is left standing alone.
