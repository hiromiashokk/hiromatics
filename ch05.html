Let's cross some lines

A quick story about crossing lines, then a couple of checks.

Two straight paths cross in the park. Right where they meet, four angles pop up around the point — let's call them a, b, c, d, going around.

∠a and ∠b sit side by side on the same straight line — together they always make 180°. That pairing has a name: a linear pair.

Look across the crossing point instead of beside it: ∠a and ∠c sit directly opposite each other. They're always exactly equal — we call them vertically opposite angles.

If all four angles happen to be equal, each one must be 90° — then the two lines are called perpendicular.

Now let one line cross two parallel lines at once. That crossing line is called a transversal, and it makes eight angles in total.

The two glowing angles sit in the exact same spot at each crossing — they're called corresponding angles, and because the lines are parallel, they're always equal.

There's one more pair worth knowing: alternate angles sit on opposite sides of the transversal, between the two lines — also always equal.

And angles on the same side, both between the lines (co-interior angles), always add up to 180°. Ready for a couple of quick checks?
