Learn

Before you tile anything, meet the big idea: shapes that fit together with no gaps and no overlaps. Grove will walk you through it.

Hi! I'm Grove.

I live in a forest of patterns. Let's learn why some shapes tile and some just... don't.

Tiling means covering a flat surface with copies of a shape so there are no gaps and no overlaps — like square tiles on a bathroom floor, or bricks in a wall.

Think of a giant chocolate bar: the little squares cover the whole bar with nothing left over. That's a tiling!

Joke: Why were the square tiles such great roommates? They always fit together — no gaps, no drama! 😄

Here's the secret rule. Look at any corner where tiles meet and spin all the way around that point — a full spin is 360°.

If the corners meeting there add up to exactly 360°, they close up perfectly. Too little leaves a gap; too much and they overlap.

Like pizza slices: if the tips add up to a full 360° turn, they make one complete pizza — no missing bites!

Which single regular shapes tile a floor by themselves? Only three:

• Triangle — 6 corners of 60° (6 × 60 = 360°)
• Square — 4 corners of 90° (4 × 90 = 360°)
• Hexagon — 3 corners of 120° (3 × 120 = 360°)

The pentagon is the odd one out: 3 corners give 3 × 108° = 324°, leaving a 36° gap. So pentagons can't tile alone.

Fun fact: Bees build honeycomb out of hexagons — the shape that holds the most honey using the least wax. Nature did the math first! 🐝

The other half of this chapter is constructions — drawing exact shapes with only a compass and ruler, no measuring.

The star trick is the perpendicular bisector: it cuts a line into two equal halves at a perfect 90°. It's like folding a paper strip exactly in half — the crease lands dead centre.

From it you can build a 90° angle, halve that for 45°, and make a 60° angle by drawing an equilateral triangle.

Fun fact: In 2023, mathematicians found a brand-new tile (nicknamed "the hat") that covers the plane forever without repeating. New math is still being found! 🎩
