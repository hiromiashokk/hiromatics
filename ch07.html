Let's master the triangle, one precise angle at a time.

A triangle has three sides, three corners, and three interior angles. No matter its shape, those angles always add up to exactly 180°.

Try it: tear the three corners off a paper triangle and line them up — they form a perfectly straight line (180°)!

Joke: Why was the obtuse triangle always upset? Because it's never right! 😄

Extend one side and you get an exterior angle. Handy rule: it equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.

So if the two far corners are 50° and 60°, the exterior angle is 110° — no need to measure it directly.

The triangle inequality: any two sides added together must be longer than the third side, or the triangle won't close up.

Two short sticks (2 cm and 3 cm) can never bridge a 6 cm gap — 2 + 3 = 5 is too short to reach!

These rules make the triangle the most reliable shape in maths — and in the real world too.

Fun fact: The triangle is the strongest shape in construction. That's why you see them in bridges, cranes and roof frames — they can't be squished out of shape! 🌉
Joke: What do you call a triangle that's always cold? An ice-sosceles! 🧊
