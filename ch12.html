Learn

A tiny dot changes everything! Let's peek just beyond the decimal point and learn to read, compare and add decimals.

Right on the dot — I'm Dot.

Let's find out what lives past the point.

After the point, places get smaller: tenths, hundredths, thousandths. So 0.7 means 7 tenths, and 0.07 means 7 hundredths.

A ruler shows this: each cm is split into 10 tiny mm, so 3.4 cm is 3 cm and 4 mm.

Joke: Why is the decimal point so calm? It always knows its place! 😄

To compare decimals, line up the points and check digit by digit. 0.5 is the same as 0.50, which beats 0.45.

Longer isn't always bigger — count the place values, not the number of digits!

To add or subtract, line up the decimal points, then work like whole numbers.

Money is the perfect example: ₹2.50 + ₹1.75 = ₹4.25. Two rupees fifty paise plus one rupee seventy-five paise.

Decimals are everywhere — prices, heights, weights, race times. 0.5 is just another way to write one half.

Fun fact: ₹1 = 100 paise, so every price tag you've ever seen is secretly a decimal in disguise! 💰
Joke: Why did the decimal break up with the fraction? They couldn't agree on their place. 💧
