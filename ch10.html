Let's ride the number line from the depths of negatives up to the positives.

Integers are the whole numbers, their negatives, and zero: …−3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3… On the number line, right of zero is positive, left is negative.

Think of a lift: ground floor is 0, the basement is −1 and −2, the floors above are +1 and +2. Or a thermometer dipping below zero on a cold morning.

Joke: Why was the negative number always sad? It could never look on the bright (positive) side! 😄

Adding is just moving along the line. Add a positive → step right. Add a negative → step left.

So 3 + (−5): start at 3, step 5 to the left, and you land on −2. Like gaining 3 points then losing 5 — you're 2 in the hole.

Subtracting a negative flips to adding: 4 − (−2) = 4 + 2 = 6. Two minuses make a plus!

It makes sense: taking away a debt actually makes you richer.

Multiplying signs follow a neat rule: same signs → positive, different signs → negative. So (−3) × (−2) = 6, but (−3) × 2 = −6.

Fun fact: The coldest temperature ever recorded on Earth was about −89°C in Antarctica — negatives are very real! 🥶
Joke: Why did −3 and +3 cancel out? They were complete opposites! ⚖️
